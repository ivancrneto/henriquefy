**Rule.** Price development work from the offer, not from your hours. A price is the intersection
of your range (minimum to maximum) with the client's; if they overlap there is a deal, and there is
no good or fair price beyond that. Your cost sets the minimum, so know it. What you can charge
above it depends on the offer: who does the work, what result you guarantee, what risk you take
(a deadline, a penalty). Selling hours with no commitment to a result leaves all the risk with the
client, who will then pay the minimum. Trading hours for money one to one is ganhar dinheiro;
creating value and taking risk is fazer dinheiro.

**Why Henrique says so.** The price formula is simple: a range from the minimum to the maximum you
want, the client's range, and a check for an intersection; if there is one there is a deal, and
there is no good price and no fair price (lesson 7, 0:28:44 to 0:29:57, `verified: false`,
transcript paraphrase). From a market hourly rate of about 70 and a market consultancy rate of 200,
he goes up: preço não é do produto, preço é da oferta; a consultancy at R$ 1000 an hour with the
country's top specialist and a guarantee of solving the problem in three months, under penalty, is
cheap for what it is (0:38:40 to 0:39:50). Charging the hour with no commitment to time or result
puts the risk on the client, who will want to pay the minimum and compare you with others
(0:43:34 to 0:43:54). Ganhar dinheiro is selling your time one to one, disconnected from value;
fazer dinheiro is creating wealth, with risk and initiative (0:01:46 to 0:02:30), and people in
tech, paid above average, get comfortable with the first (0:04:40 to 0:05:11). Digest sections 6.9
and 6.10.

**Looks like.** A fixed-price proposal for a stated result, with a deadline and what happens if it
is missed, priced against what the result is worth to the client and never below your cost. Bad:
an hourly quote matched to the cheapest competitor, with no idea of your own monthly cost.

**How to detect.** No code signal. `ask` mode only: questions about freelancing rates,
consulting, proposals, salary negotiation.

**How to fix.** Compute your cost per hour as the floor. Ask what the result is worth to the
client. Write the offer: the result, the deadline, the guarantee and the risk you take, and price
it inside the overlap.
