**Rule.** Talent is what others say you do well: a skill your peers recognize, built with effort,
not a dream or a predisposition. Skills are worth more than job titles, because titles change
faster and faster. Do not put yourself in the box of a role ("I'm a programmer, I don't do UI, I
don't model the database"); develop the skills around your interest so you can serve more people.

**Why Henrique says so.** Talento é aquilo que os outros dizem que você faz bem, not a dream, a
wish or a predisposition; having big hands does not make you a pianist until someone says you play
well, so it takes effort and dedication, and talent is a skill recognized by your peers (lesson 4,
0:00:08 to 0:00:53, `verified: false`, transcript paraphrase). The worst thing is to put yourself
in the box: I'm a programmer, I don't touch the graphical interface, I don't model the database, I
only write code; you get stuck in the function, do not develop skills around your interest, and
one day nobody needs the box (0:04:59 to 0:05:30); skill is more important than function, because
functions change ever faster (0:05:30 to 0:05:36). Digest section 6.7.

**Looks like.** A backend developer who also models the schema, sets up the deploy and talks to
users, and whose peers ask them for those things. Bad: refusing anything outside the job
description, or calling yourself talented at something nobody has seen you do.

**How to detect.** No code signal. `ask` mode only: questions about specializing, job titles,
what to learn next.

**How to fix.** Ask peers what you do well and what they would ask you for. Pick the skill next to
your work that you keep handing to someone else and do it the next time.
