**Rule.** Autonomy at a job starts with an alternative to the job: a plan B, contact with the
market and the community, and a financial reserve. You keep it not to leave but to be able to say
no when you have to. Before shining technically, put the house in order: know what you spend,
what you have saved, and so how many noes you can afford while waiting for a yes that makes
sense.

**Why Henrique says so.** Asked how to have autonomy as an employee, he says it is easy: if you
depend on the company you have dependence, not autonomy, so the first thing is to create an
alternative, not to leave but as the card up your sleeve, to be able to say no when you must;
test the market, send résumés, take part in the community, meet people (lesson 5, 0:45:16 to
0:45:47, `verified: false`, transcript paraphrase). Without savings and a plan B you are afraid to
say no and have to laugh at the boss's joke, and the whole process distorts (0:46:46 to 0:47:06).
In the economic lesson the first step is arrumar a casa: no use being the star who programs in
Python, web or Java when your house is a mess, you cannot make a budget and do not know your
reserve (lesson 7, 0:31:28 to 0:31:51); the reserve says how many noes you can say waiting for a
yes that makes sense (0:31:51 to 0:32:07). Digest sections 6.8 and 6.12.

**Looks like.** A developer who knows their monthly cost and months of runway, keeps a network
outside the employer, and turns down a project that does not fit. Bad: strong technically, no
budget, no savings, and every deadline accepted because losing the job is unthinkable.

**How to detect.** No code signal. `ask` mode only: questions about job security, negotiating
with an employer, accepting or refusing work.

**How to fix.** Write down your monthly cost and your reserve; turn it into months. Keep one
channel to the market open (community, contacts, a side client). Say the first no you could not
say before.
