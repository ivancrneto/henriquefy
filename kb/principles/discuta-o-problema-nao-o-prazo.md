**Rule.** When someone asks for a date, do not haggle over the date. Ask when they need it and
why, set the project aside, and work on the problem behind the date. Then make the solution fit
the time: ask whether everything fits, cut the least important part, and repeat until it does,
keeping the cut work in order for later instead of throwing it away.

**Why Henrique says so.** Faced with a requested deadline, he asks for when and why (Método para
Cumprir Qualquer Prazo, lesson 7, 0:25:50 to 0:26:34), forgets the project and focuses on the
problem (0:26:34 to 0:26:44), and from then on he no longer discusses the deadline, he discusses
the problem, and the solution has to fit the deadline (0:27:36 to 0:27:51, `verified: false`,
transcript paraphrase). He does not ask what can be done in a week; he asks whether everything
can be done, then what is least important, and repeats for shorter deadlines (lesson 4, 0:24:19
to 0:25:29), without discarding the rest and prioritizing the sequence (0:28:35 to 0:28:42). In
the course's case, the first release is cut down to catalog, search and direct sale (lesson 7,
0:18:39 to 0:19:12).

**Looks like.** "You need it by the 30th for the trade fair? Then what has to work at the fair is
taking orders; reports and the admin come after." Bad: answering "impossible, I need two more
weeks" and negotiating the date while the scope stays whole.

**How to detect.** Judgment, in plans and conversations: a deadline discussion with no stated
reason for the date; estimates negotiated down without anything taken out of scope.

**How to fix.** Ask what the date is for, restate the problem in one sentence, list the scope by
importance to that problem, and cut from the bottom until it fits. Related:
`pronto-e-quando-esta-no-ar`, `eficacia-acima-de-eficiencia`, `postergue-decisoes`.
