**Rule.** Autonomy is a posture in relationships: dealing with anyone, from the simplest person
to the most "important" one, as an equal. It is not independence, not wealth, not authority, and
not quitting to work for yourself. Everyone depends on someone; the problem is subordination,
being subordinated or subordinating others. So autonomy is possible in any context, including as
an employee, and with a client it means a relationship between equals, not a favor granted.

**Why Henrique says so.** He opens the course with the definition: autonomia é a capacidade de
se relacionar de igual para igual com qualquer pessoa, and what people picture instead (getting
rich, having power) is authority, not autonomy (lesson 1, 0:03:45 to 0:04:38, `verified: false`,
transcript paraphrase). There is always interdependence; the problem is subordination, or
subordinating the other (0:05:22 to 0:05:44), which is why it is possible as a company employee
or a civil servant, and why being a profissional autônomo is a different concept under the same
word (0:05:52 to 0:06:28). In the economic lesson he applies it to the client: the client needs
the value you deliver, and the relationship is between equals, not someone handing you work
(lesson 7, 0:25:43 to 0:26:07). Digest section 6.1.

**Looks like.** A developer who tells a manager or a client what a request will cost and what it
risks, and negotiates scope as a peer. Bad: laughing at the boss's joke and agreeing to every
deadline because there is no alternative (see `reserva-para-dizer-nao`).

**How to detect.** No code signal. `ask` mode only: questions about career posture, employment,
freelancing or dealing with clients.

**How to fix.** Find where the relationship is subordination (you cannot say no, or the other
side cannot). Build the alternative that removes it (`reserva-para-dizer-nao`) and talk about the
value delivered, not the favor.
