**Rule.** Leadership is not a title. To lead is to take responsibility for the result and to
remove the obstacles in your peers' way, whether or not anyone gave you authority.

**Why Henrique says so.** Leading is reducing other people's obstacles, with no position required
(Método para Cumprir Qualquer Prazo, lesson 5, 0:02:03 to 0:02:39, `verified: false`, transcript
paraphrase), taking responsibility for the result even without authority (0:08:10 to 0:08:27;
0:16:58 to 0:17:12). In the course's case, an experiment with Django removes obstacles and attacks
a risk (lesson 6, 0:10:55 to 0:11:18), and in front of the nervous client he takes the heat
himself (lesson 7, 0:04:03 to 0:04:19).

**Looks like.** A developer who sees the team blocked on a flaky staging environment fixes it, or
gets it fixed, before anyone asks. Bad: "that's not my job, the tech lead decides"; a lead who
assigns tasks and tracks them but never clears what is stopping them.

**How to detect.** Judgment: work stuck on the same blocker across standups with nobody owning
it; responsibility for a result pushed to whoever has the title.

**How to fix.** Ask each peer what is stopping them, take the obstacle that most threatens the
result, and report it removed. Related: `habilidade-acima-de-funcao`,
`autonomia-e-relacao-entre-iguais`.
